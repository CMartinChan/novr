NOVR · VR HUD design pass 0.1 · 2026-09-27

NOVR HUD redesign

A proposal for how Nuclear Option's HUD, helmet display, map and panels should sit in VR. Every size and position is given in degrees of your view instead of desktop pixels, so it looks the same on any monitor and any headset.

Code base upstream dev @ 2a0f789Game Nuclear Option 0.34.2Measured on 3840 × 2160 desktop, Rift S (Oculus runtime)Status proposal, nothing implemented yet

In short

What's wrong

The HUD is laid out in desktop reference pixels and scaled by your desktop window height. At 3840 × 2160 every HUD element is drawn twice as large as at 1080p. The pitch ladder is a full ring around you. Weapons, countermeasures and damage data sit 14–39° off the nose, beyond comfortable eye movement.

The idea

Pin every element to one of four frames (world, aircraft, head or cockpit panel) and size it in visual angle. Flight and weapon data fit in a 26° × 23° window on the nose, readable by moving only your eyes. Text is 0.6–0.9° tall, lines are 0.12° wide, and the ladder is conformal (1:1 with the real horizon).

Map and panels

The minimap, damage display and messages move onto cockpit-fixed panels. The minimap and damage display sit at instrument-panel depth about 27° below the nose, beside the target camera, which already lives on a cockpit screen. The full map opens as a cockpit-fixed tablet at arm's length. It stays in the cockpit and doesn't follow your head or the HUD.

See it from the seat

Proposed layout, drawn to the angular spec below. Pick a situation, then turn your head with the sliders. World-fixed markers stay on their targets. The HUD stays with the aircraft, the helmet data follows your head, and the panels stay in the cockpit.

What's wrong today

Findings from reading NOVR's dev branch against the game's own HUD classes and your settings. Angles are for your 3840 × 2160 desktop. At 1080p the canvas-based ones halve. Items marked "est." still need confirming in-game.

Root cause

Scale follows the desktop window, not the headset

NOVR switches the game's HUD canvas to world space and puts it 1,000 m ahead, but keeps the scale the game's CanvasScaler gave it: desktop height ÷ 1080. One layout pixel is 0.057° at 1080p and 0.115° at your 2160p. Your HUD text setting (40) comes out around 3.2° tall (est.). A real HUD character is about 0.5°, and VR text reads comfortably at about 0.7–1°.

Evidence: VrHudProjectionHelper.GetScreenHeightScale() converts with 1080 / Screen.height. UIRenderedCanvasBehavior never resets localScale. Registry: Screenmanager Resolution Height = 2160, HUDTextSize = 40.

Pitch ladder

The ladder is a ring around you, drawn at desktop size

PitchCompassBehavior slices the ladder texture into 36 five-degree bands and places them, plus a mirrored copy, on a full circle through the nose, over your head and behind you. The rung positions are correct. The rung art, though, is drawn at 0.8 × the canvas scale (1.6× the desktop art at your resolution). The ladder is centred on the nose instead of the flight path marker, with no HUD field of view to stop it. On dev the cockpit now hides it, which helps.

Placement

Weapon, countermeasure and damage data sit outside easy eye movement

At your resolution the weapon panel sits about 28° right and 27° up, and the countermeasures about 14° left and 27° up. The lower-left panel is about 39° left and the damage display about 32° right. Most natural eye movements are 15° or less, and beyond about 20° your head joins in (see research). Turning your head to read a number is slow in a fight.

Evidence: hard-coded offsets in NOVRFlightHudBehavior (TopRightPanel 330, 290 × 0.6; LowerLeftPanel −400, 80) and NOVRStatusDisplayBehavior (630, 145, 1000), multiplied by the canvas scale of 2.

Depth

The full map, messages and spawn menus share one 88° × 57° plane, 3 m ahead

GameplayUI (messages, dialogue, airbase and aircraft selection, leaderboard), MessageUI, MenuCanvas and MaximizedMapCanvas are all placed 3 m ahead at 3 mm per layout pixel. Their corners reach 44° sideways and 28° up and down. At 3 m they sit behind the instrument panel (about 0.7 m away) but are drawn on top of it. Your eyes get contradictory depth cues wherever the two overlap. The minimap stays in a corner of its desktop-sized canvas (position to confirm in-game). The target camera is fine: it already renders to the aircraft's own cockpit screen.

Helmet data

Head-locked data is always on and duplicates the HUD

NOVRHMDBehavior follows your smoothed gaze and pins speed, altitude, heading and a horizon ball about 12–22° above the middle of your view. It stays on when the HUD is right in front of you and when you look down into the cockpit. The game's own helmet display settings (side distance and angle, top height) are overridden with fixed pixel offsets.

Markers

Unit markers keep desktop pixel sizes

Markers are placed correctly on their units, since their direction is projected properly. Their icons and labels, though, keep the canvas's pixel size (icon size 30 is about 3.4° at your resolution, est.), and every marker gets the same treatment regardless of distance or relevance.

What real HUDs do, and what VR changes

Condensed from military symbology standards, flight manuals, headset specs and VR design guides. Sources are listed at the end.

What real HUDs and helmet displays do

  • Everything is collimated. Symbology is a virtual image at optical infinity, so the ladder is conformal: 5° of rung spacing covers 5° of sky. Placement tolerance is ±1.7 mrad on axis.
  • The window is 20–30° wide. Total field of view is 20° for the F/A-18 and F-15C, 25° for the F-16C (20° × 13.5° from one eye position), 30° × 22° for the Rafale and 30° × 25° for the Typhoon. Symbols that reach the edge stop there and change form. The Hornet's velocity vector stops 8° from centre and flashes.
  • One layout across aircraft. The heading tape (compressed, not 1:1) is at the top, airspeed on the left and altitude on the right, with a data block under each. The left block holds Mach, G, peak G and AoA. The right holds radar altitude, vertical velocity and range. Weapon, count and master arm sit low, the launch-zone scale sits on the right, and a bank scale runs along the bottom.
  • Ladder conventions. Rungs come every 5°, solid above the horizon and dashed below, with numbers at the rung ends that roll with the ladder. Negative rungs bend toward the horizon at half the angle (Hornet, F-16C/D), which tested best for unusual-attitude recovery. There's a long horizon line and a dashed ghost horizon once the real one leaves view. Compression is used only in a dedicated unusual-attitude mode.
  • Sizes. The handbook minimum for HUD characters is 24 arcminutes (0.40°). Lines are about 1 mrad (0.057°) wide, and a stroke is 0.12–0.2 × the character height. Weapon cues run 8–56 mrad (0.5–3.2°). No public source gives the flight path marker's size, but about 1° fits the cues around it.
  • Helmet displays use three frames. Head-fixed data (G, speed, altitude, heading, target range) sits around an aiming cross, target boxes and steerpoints are world-stabilized, and a virtual HUD is aircraft-fixed. JHMCS blanks when you look through the HUD, and below the canopy rail it keeps only target symbols. In a NASA study, pilots misjudged pitch three times as often with a conformal horizon in the helmet and preferred a body-axis attitude display.
  • Colour and clutter. HUDs use P43 green (about 544 nm). Colour helmets show adversaries red and friendlies blue. Red means warning and amber means caution. The Hornet's declutter levels first strip Mach, G and the box outlines, then the heading scale. FAA guidance keeps display density under 50%, ideally 25% or less.
  • Head-down displays carry the map, radar, radar warning and targeting-pod video, 25–35° below the eye line. The F-35 uses a 20 × 8 in panoramic screen split into portals. A head-up to head-down glance costs about 0.7 s.

Why VR text must be bigger than a real HUD's: the 0.40° minimum assumes about 1 arcminute of visual acuity. A headset pixel is 2.4 arcminutes (Quest 3) to 4.4 (Index), so the same legibility needs text about 1.5–2× larger.

What VR changes

  • Sharpness is a third to a half of normal vision. Quest 3 resolves about 25 pixels per degree, Quest Pro 22, Quest 2 and Pico 4 about 20, and Index about 13–14. Bigscreen Beyond reaches 32 and Varjo Aero 35 at its peak. 20/20 vision is about 60. These are lens-centre best cases, and resampling and reprojection lose another 25–50%.
  • Text needs at least 10 pixels of height. That means a cap height of at least 0.40° on Quest 3, 0.50° on Quest 2 and 0.74° on Index. Guidance converges on at least 0.7° em for numbers and at least 1.0° em for reading text on a 20-PPD headset.
  • Lines need at least 2 pixels: about 0.1° on Quest and 0.15° on Index. A real HUD's 1-milliradian (0.057°) line is too thin and shimmers.
  • Eyes cover about 15°, then the head joins in. Most natural eye movements are 15° or less, and beyond about 20° the head moves too. Comfortable head yaw is about ±30°, pitch about 20° up and 12° down. Resting gaze sits 10–20° below the horizon.
  • Distance. Quest 2's lenses focus at about 1.3 m. Oculus recommends 0.75–3.5 m for interface. Stereo depth fades by 10–20 m, and at 100 m the disparity is under a pixel, so 1,000 m is effectively infinity.
  • Anchoring. Microsoft, Meta and Apple all warn against rigidly head-locked interface. A cockpit is a stable rest frame. Head-locked content drawn into the eye buffer judders under Meta's spacewarp, so it needs smoothing or a compositor layer.
  • Depth conflicts. Symbology at infinity painted over nearer geometry contradicts occlusion. The fixes in use are to clip symbology to the combiner (a DCS mod), blank the helmet display over the HUD and cockpit (JHMCS), and put panels at their surface's depth.

What Nuclear Option already does

  • The game treats its HUD as helmet symbology. Since 0.29.1, speed and attitude are view-locked. Extra helmet elements appear when you look away from the nose, controlled by Distance to hide HMD (yours is 0.33). The proposal keeps that behaviour but measures it as an angle.
  • Look-to-select. A designator diamond at the centre of view selects the unit under it. Version 0.34 added a chevron on the closest aircraft and names the unit nearest the cursor.
  • Weapon cues already sit where real ones do. The range scale (MAX, NEZ, MIN and a TGT arrow) is on the right, with SHOOT or X plus a reason. Bombing uses CCRP with the nose above the horizon and CCIP below it. Landing uses a glideslope line and an AoA indexer.
  • Threats show as a flashing missile icon, a threat list above the minimap and a grey, yellow or red radar-detection bar. A yellow dotted notch line appears on both the HUD and the map.
  • Desktop players already report target clutter near the ground and a weak weapon and countermeasure readout (they ask for bottom centre). The HUD is hard to read near the sun and clouds, and there are no colour options.
  • NOVR players praise that the UI renders at infinity and like look-to-target. They report chat width (#41), the cursor on a different plane from buttons (#40), a map behind the player on Pimax, and a dim HUD. The dann1kid fork (0.4.6–0.4.8) already tried a narrow ladder, data near the nose and a smaller off-boresight ring, the same direction as this proposal.

The proposal

Six rules, four frames and one window. The mockups above follow these rules.

Rules

  • Degrees, not pixels. Every size and offset is a visual angle, converted with size = 2·d·tan(θ/2) at the element's distance d. Nothing reads Screen.height.
  • Eyes before head. Anything you need in a fight sits within about 13° of the nose. Occasional information sits 20–30° off the nose, where a glance reaches it. Nothing important needs a head turn.
  • Conformal means 1:1. The flight path marker, ladder, horizon, target boxes, pippers, runway cues and markers point at the real thing they represent.
  • Infinity for symbols, panel depth for panels. Symbology renders at 1,000 m (optical infinity) and is hidden by cockpit structure. Panels sit at the instrument panel's depth or nearer, never behind geometry they are drawn over.
  • A legibility floor. Text is at least 0.6° tall and lines at least 0.12° wide, with a dark halo so green reads against bright cloud.
  • Declutter by relevance. The selected target, threats, the objective and nearby friendlies get labels. Everything else is a small icon, dimmer with distance.

Four frames

The HUD window, dimensioned

Air-to-air layout at the spec's exact angles. Yellow rulers are in degrees from the nose. The window is 26° wide (−13° to +13°) and 23° tall (−12° to +11°), which is about a real wide-angle HUD's total field of view.

Type, line and symbol scale

TokenAngleQuest 3Quest 2Rift S, IndexUsed for

Pixel heights assume centre sharpness of about 25 (Quest 3), 20 (Quest 2) and 14 (Rift S and Index; the Rift S figure is estimated from 1,280 px across roughly 88°) pixels per degree. Legible text needs about 10 pixels of height, which every token clears on Quest. On your Rift S, T3 lands at 8.4 px and a stroke at 1.7 px. So low-sharpness headsets get a 1.2× text scale and 0.15° strokes by default. A HUD text scale setting (0.8–1.5×) covers other headsets and personal preference. Real HUDs use about 0.40° characters and 0.057° lines, which is too fine for today's headsets.

Colour and declutter

  • HUD colour comes from the game's setting (yours is pure green). A 0.05° dark halo sits behind every stroke and glyph so symbology survives bright cloud and snow. Real HUDs can't do this, but VR can.
  • Meaning colours only: amber for caution (low countermeasures, fuel, damage), red for warnings (missile inbound, pull up), faction blue and red for markers. The HUD window never shows more than three hues at once.
  • Labels go only on the selected target, active threats, the objective and friendlies within 5 km, with at most 6 at once. Other markers are icons, drawn 0.75° and at 50% opacity beyond 40 km.
  • Declutter key cycles FULL → NO GAUGES → FIGHT, modelled on the Hornet's NORM, REJ 1 and REJ 2. FIGHT keeps the flight path marker, ladder, target, weapon, launch zone and warnings. Invalid data is flagged, never silently hidden, and the window aims to stay under 25% ink.
  • Stale contacts (the game's "?" last-known positions) are drawn dashed at half opacity, so they can't be mistaken for live tracks.
  • Landing config (gear down) hides weapons, countermeasures and the launch zone and shows the AoA bracket and gear state.

The map and panels

You asked whether the map should move with the HUD. The recommendation is no. The map belongs to the cockpit, like a real MFD or a kneeboard.

Why cockpit-fixed

  • Not with the HUD's world layer. Conformal symbology moves with the outside world at 500 kt. A map pinned there would slide across the canopy as you turn.
  • Not head-locked. A 50° panel glued to your view hides the outside world wherever you look. Microsoft, Meta and Apple all flag rigidly head-locked content as a discomfort trigger. MSFS players complain about exactly this with its floating windows. Try the Head-locked option in the Map scenario and turn your head.
  • Cockpit-fixed. The cockpit is your rest frame in VR. Content fixed to it feels solid, you can glance away and back, and a mouse or controller ray hits a stable target.

Where each panel goes

  • Minimap: left MFD, 18° left and 27.5° down, 16° × 15° on the panel face at about 0.7 m. That's the size of a real 8-inch MFD. It keeps the existing opacity setting.
  • Target camera: it already renders onto the aircraft's own cockpit screen, so it is at the right depth today. Keep it there (the mockup shows it on the right MFD). Some cockpit screens are small, so holding a key can show an enlarged 30° repeater beside the HUD (+24°, −6°).
  • Damage and systems: centre panel, 30.5° down. A new failure also flashes a short amber caution in the HUD at −7°.
  • Comms (kill feed, messages, chat): a strip left of the HUD at eye level (−26°, +6°), rendered at infinity in the canopy glass. Lines fade after 10 s, so it's usually empty.
  • Full map: opens as a 50° × 30° tablet 12° below the nose at about 0.7 m, with its lower edge tilted toward you so it sits just in front of the instrument panel face. That is at the near edge of comfortable VR viewing (Meta suggests 0.75 m or more). It's acceptable for a view you open briefly. Farther away it would sit behind the panel and need the cockpit dimmed. The header repeats speed, heading, altitude and any missile warning, and the top of the HUD stays visible above it.
  • Recentring is measured against the cockpit's forward direction, never the headset's playspace. A playspace-relative map rendered behind a Pimax user.
  • One cursor surface. Every interactive panel takes mouse and controller rays on its own plane. Today the cursor can sit on a different plane from a button (#40).

How to build it

Phased so each step ships on its own and can go upstream as a pull request to dev. File names are on dev.

PHASE 0

Measure in-game

In a free flight, use NOVR's local inspector to record the real sizes and positions of the FlightHud, HUDCenter and HMDCenter elements and the canvas scale. That confirms the "est." numbers above and gives before-and-after baselines. About an hour.

PHASE 1

Degrees, not pixels

The biggest win for the least code. About 2–3 evenings.

  • Add a small HudAngles helper (degrees to world units at a given distance) and a HudLayout table holding the positions in this document.
  • UIRenderedCanvasBehavior: set world-space canvas scale explicitly, independent of Screen.height. Remove GetScreenHeightScale from VrHudProjectionHelper.
  • NOVRFlightHudBehavior, NOVRHMDBehavior: move speed, altitude, heading, weapons and countermeasures into the HUD window.
  • ModConfiguration: HUD Text Scale, HUD Window Offset.
PHASE 2

A real pitch ladder

About 3–4 evenings.

  • Replace the texture slices in PitchCompassBehavior with rungs, ticks and labels built from lines and text. Centre them on the flight path marker, bend the negative rungs, and add the horizon.
  • Keep it on the clipped layer from NOUIManager.ClippedHudCamera so the cockpit hides it. Add a window mask (stencil or UI mask) for the 26° × 23° window.
PHASE 3

Cockpit panels and the map tablet

About a week of evenings.

  • New NOVRCockpitPanel component: a world-space canvas at a cockpit-fixed pose with its own depth rules. Use it for the minimap (DynamicMap.hudMapAnchor), StatusDisplay and MessageUI. Leave TargetCam on its cockpit screen and add the optional enlarged repeater.
  • Full map tablet: summon on open, recentre key, keep NOVRDynamicMapBehavior and VrCanvasHitTester clicks working with mouse and controller.
PHASE 4

Helmet layer and declutter

About 3–4 evenings.

  • NOVRHMDBehavior: fade by angle off the nose, hide below −20° and with the map open. Add target and threat arrows.
  • HUDUnitMarkerPatch: angular icon sizes, distance fade, label rules. ThreatItemPatch: HUD ring arrow and a shorter notch line.
PHASE 5

Polish

  • Halo material for HUD strokes and text, headset presets for text scale, settings in NativeVrUiSettingsPanel.
  • Check upstream's unmerged feature/screenspace-ztest-always-ui branch (static, HMD and smoothed-HMD "armatures"). It is the same four-frame idea, so agree the direction with the maintainer before Phase 3.

Open questions for you

  1. Headset. Your vault and logs point to a Rift S, which resolves about 14 px/deg. The spec's base sizes target 20–25 px/deg headsets, so on the Rift S the text scale defaults to 1.2×. Are you planning a newer headset, or should the Rift S be the baseline everything is tuned on?
  2. HUD window size. 26° × 23° matches a real wide-angle HUD. Would you prefer it slightly larger (30° × 26°) at the cost of more eye movement?
  3. Comms placement. Left of the HUD at eye level (proposed), or low on the right MFD edge?
  4. Game settings versus NOVR settings. Keep reading the game's HUD colour and text size, or give NOVR its own VR values and ignore the desktop ones?
  5. Map summon. Should the full map always open 13° below the nose, or open wherever you're looking?
